Blockchain Research Prototype

VoteSphere India

A blockchain-enabled election transparency, EPIC voter-identity verification and privacy-preserving digital ballot simulation platform.

Independent educational prototype. Not affiliated with the Election Commission of India. All voters, candidates and results are fictional.

The problem

Voters need proof that ballots are counted, yet ballots must stay secret and identity checks must not leak personal data. VoteSphere demonstrates how identity, ballots and audit evidence can be kept in separate, verifiable layers.

Our approach

Mock electoral-roll verification, one-time voting authorizations, AES-256-GCM ballot encryption, hash commitments with unique nullifiers on a local ledger, and a tally that re-checks every commitment.

Journey of a ballot

1
Verify EPIC
Roll lookup returns one of five statuses. OCR is never proof of identity.
2
Login with OTP
Hashed password, rate limiting and expiring sessions.
3
One-time authorization
Consumed on use, so replays and duplicate votes are blocked.
4
Encrypt ballot
AES-GCM ciphertext. The key never sits beside the data.
5
Commit on chain
Only a hash commitment and nullifier are public. No names, no votes.
6
Tally and publish
Chain integrity, commitments, ties and seat totals are all checked.

Separated storage

Identity store
Citizen records, hashed credentials, voted flag. Never holds a selection.
Ballot store
Encrypted ballots only, with no link to a person.
Ledger
Hash-chained commitments, nullifiers and election events.

Working features

  • EPIC verification with five statuses
  • Registration, OTP login, role-based admin
  • Candidate and party directories
  • Encrypted ballots, duplicate and replay blocking
  • Tamper detection, tie detection, seat totals
  • National results dashboard

Technology

Running prototype: browser app with Web Crypto (SHA-256, AES-GCM, PBKDF2) and a local hash-chain ledger.

Target architecture: React, TypeScript, Node, PostgreSQL, Prisma, Solidity, Hardhat, Ethers.js, OpenZeppelin, Argon2id.

Limitations

Verification uses a mock service with synthetic records; no ECI integration exists. Tallying relies on a trusted key holder. This is a research demonstration and must never be used for a real election.
